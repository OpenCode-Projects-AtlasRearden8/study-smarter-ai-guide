# Chapter 3 — Spaced Review (So Things Actually Stick)

*Study Smarter with AI*

## The problem: forgetting is normal

You studied hard. Two weeks later it's gone. That's not a character flaw — that's the **forgetting curve**, and it's completely normal. What's *not* normal is fighting it with brute force, cramming the night before, and re-learning the same material from scratch every semester.

The fix is wonderfully lazy: **review each topic right when you're about to forget it.** Reviewing material you're about to forget is worth many times more than re-reading material you already know cold. This is called **spaced repetition**, and it's the closest thing education has to a cheat code.

## The simplest schedule that works (no apps needed)

For each batch of notes, review:

- **Day 0** — learn it
- **Day 2** — first quick quiz (5 minutes)
- **Day 7** — second quiz (10 minutes)
- **Day 21** — third quiz (10 minutes)
- **Before the exam** — one final pass, focusing on what you got wrong

That's it. Four touchpoints and the material moves from "I saw it" to "I own it." The AI's role: **make day 2, 7, and 21 trivially easy to run.**

## Ask AI to be your review scheduler

```
Here are my notes about [topic]. Design me a 3-week spaced
review plan. Each review should take 10 minutes or less and
be a short quiz plus one explain-it-back question. Give me
a checklist format I can copy into my phone.
```

The magic is that **day 7 and day 21 quizzes can be different from day 2's**. On day 21, the gaps you fell into on day 2 and 7 are exactly what the AI should hammer:

```
Here's my review plan and the topics I got wrong last time
([list them]). Quiz me mostly on those, plus a few new
questions from my notes.
```

Now the schedule adapts to *your* brain instead of a generic one. That's the whole advantage of doing this with AI versus a printed checklist.

## Where to keep it

Honest advice: the tool you'll actually use beats the fancy one. In ascending order of commitment:

1. **Calendar events.** "Quiz — biology notes, 10 min." Works for everyone. Truly works.
2. **A running learning journal.** One markdown file or note app document. Each entry: date, topic, what you got wrong. Ask AI to read the journal and tell you what to quiz yourself on next.
3. **Dedicated flashcard apps** (many free — see Chapter 6) that automate the scheduling. Great once you have a steady flow of material.

Start at level 1. Move up only when it feels natural, not because a tool would be "nice."

## Fitting reviews into real life

- **Attach reviews to existing habits.** Quiz while coffee brews. On the bus. Walking to class. Small and done beats perfect and scheduled.
- **If you miss a review, do it late.** Late spaced review still works; the curve hasn't collapsed, it's just steeper.
- **Keep reviews SHORT.** Never longer than 10–15 minutes each. Spaced repetition earns its keep through repetition, not through marathon sessions.

## Try it today

Pick your last two weeks of study material. Set three calendar events: one in two days, one in a week, one in three weeks, each titled "Review: [topic] + AI quiz." That took you four minutes and just made your future self measurably smarter.
