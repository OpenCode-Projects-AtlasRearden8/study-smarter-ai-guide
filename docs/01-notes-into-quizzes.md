# Chapter 1 — Turning Your Notes Into Quizzes

*Study Smarter with AI*

## Why quizzing works

Here's a finding that surprises a lot of people: **re-reading your notes feels productive but isn't.** Reading something again just makes it *familiar*, and familiarity is not the same as knowing. What actually builds memory is **retrieval practice** — the act of pulling an answer out of your head. Every time you dig up a fact from memory, your brain marks it as "worth keeping."

Quizzing is retrieval practice with a lid on it. And AI is honestly great at writing the quizzes, because writing good questions is tedious — but AI doesn't get tired, and it can generate 20 practice questions from your notes in the time it takes you to sharpen a pencil.

## The basic recipe

Take a chunk of your notes — roughly one lecture's worth, not the whole semester — and paste it into a chat with an AI. Then use a prompt like this one:

```
Here are my class notes. Act like a friendly quiz master.

1. Ask me ONE question at a time and wait for my answer.
2. After I answer, tell me if I was right, then briefly
   explain the correct answer.
3. Ask follow-up "why" questions when it would help.
4. After 10 questions, give me a score and tell me which
   topics I was weakest on.
5. Any false starts I make, be encouraging — I'm learning.

Notes: [paste your notes here]
```

That's the whole trick. One question at a time matters; if the AI dumps 20 questions at once, you'll skim them — and skimming is the re-reading trap again.

## Dialing the difficulty

A few follow-up phrases that make the quiz fit you better:

- **"Ask me mostly why questions, not definitions."** Definitions test memory; *why* questions test understanding. You want a mix, weighted toward why.
- **"Ask me the questions I'm most likely to see on an exam."** If you have a study guide or practice exam, paste that too — "make my quiz in the same style as these questions."
- **"I got that wrong — ask me a similar one in a minute."** This is the AI version of the spaced-review idea from Chapter 3, done in miniature.
- **"Play a character."** For example: "Pretend you're a patient and I'm the nurse — I have to explain my medications to you in normal words." This works absurdly well and is a lot less boring than a plain quiz.

## Turn your flashcards into AI flashcards

If your notes live in flashcard apps or a spreadsheet, even better. Try:

```
Here are my flashcard fronts and backs. Quiz me on these,
one at a time, shuffled. When I get one wrong, end by asking
me to restate the answer in my own words.
```

And if you *don't* have flashcards yet, ask the AI:

```
From these notes, make me 15 flashcards. Format each one as
FRONT: question / BACK: answer. Keep backs under 25 words.
```

(Then check them against your notes — see Chapter 4 on hallucinations. Occasionally AI "remembers" a detail your instructor never said.)

## A gentle warning

AI-generated quizzes are a study aid, not a source of truth about what will be on your exam. Your instructor's vibe, the textbook's emphasis, the specific wording of past exams — AI doesn't know those things unless you tell it. Treat AI quizzes as *warm-ups*, and your instructor's materials as the real game film.

## Try it today

Open your messiest set of notes. Paste them into any AI chat with the quiz-master prompt above. Answer ten questions. Congratulations — that's real studying, with a robot holding a clipboard. Be kind to yourself about the score; the score is information, not judgment.
