Chapter 6 — Free Stuff That Actually Works

Study Smarter with AI

You can run this whole guide's playbook for $0. Here's the honest map of the free landscape, and where each piece fits.

Free AI chats (your study partner)

Most major AI services have solid free tiers, usable in any browser without installing anything. This guide stays product-agnostic — services change pricing and names all the time. What to evaluate:

  • Daily limits: free tiers limit how many messages you get per day (or per few hours). Enough for the techniques in this guide; you'll notice limits only with heavy daily use.
  • File uploads: can you attach a photo of your handwritten notes, or a PDF? Methods matter less than the technique; a quiz-master prompt works with either.
  • Accounts: any free account on any major service works for Chapters 1–5.

The key insight: the technique matters far more than the tool. A great prompt in a modest free chat beats a lazy prompt in the fanciest model. Prompt quality is the free upgrade.

Free flashcards and spaced repetition (your memory system)

Chapter 3's schedule can be run in a paper calendar — but if you want automation, these are the free workhorses (all run in a browser, most with phone apps):

  • Anki (apps.ankiweb.net) — the veteran spaced-repetition app. Free on desktop, web, and Android; the iOS app is paid (the web version works on iPhone for free). It schedules reviews for you using the science from Chapter 3. Steeper learning curve, unmatched depth.
  • Quizlet (quizlet.com) — friendliest starting point, familiar interface, strong free features with some content behind a subscription.
  • Cold Turkey-style note apps — any notes app with checkboxes works for the journal approach in Chapter 3.

A workflow that ties it together: have AI generate flashcards from your notes in FRONT/BACK text format (Chapter 1), paste them into your flashcard app, and let the app schedule reviews while the AI handles explain-it-back sessions.

Free tools for making and sharing what you learn

  • GitHub (github.com) — free hosting for notes, flashcard text files, and anything you build. Your study materials, versioned and backed up. The hub site for this guide's author lives there too — see dylanasdale.github.io.
  • Static site tools — if you write notes in Markdown, free tools (many listed in this repo's build script — check build.py) can turn them into a shareable website like... well, like this guide.
  • Library databases — your real superpower. Your school or public library gives you licensed, verified sources: the exact thing to check AI answers against (Chapters 4 and 5). Librarians will happily show you — it's their favorite conversation.

What "free" costs, honestly

Free tiers have limits; ads exist; some features are paid. Two rules of thumb:

  1. Never pay to access basic studying. Everything in this guide — quizzes, explain-back, spaced review — works fine on free tiers.
  2. If a tool asks for your data, read what it wants. Prefer tools that let you study without selling your study habits. When in doubt, the plain-browser free tiers of the major AI services are the least surprising.

The anti-list: things you don't need

  • A paid "AI tutor" service with a fancy landing page — the free chats do this.
  • Premium note-taking apps before you have a note habit. Blank paid apps hold zero notes.
  • Any tool claiming to "learn for you." That's Chapter 5's escalator in product form.

Try it today

Create one free account — a flashcard app or an AI chat, whichever you don't have. Do one thing in it from this guide. One account, one technique, ten minutes. That's a complete start.
